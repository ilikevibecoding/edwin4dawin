## 1. The idea behind the pass

Baseline hostiles were competent but transparent: they walked at you in the open, shot until the
magazine ran dry, and each one fought its own private war. Everything below is aimed at one thing —
making the *reason* for a hostile's next move legible to the player. You should be able to watch a
firefight and narrate it: that one is pinned, that one is coming around the left, that one heard the
first shout and is still on its way.

Four decisions shape the implementation:

**Cover is data, not a search.** Cover points are extracted once at bake time from the collider
list, so combat only ever does a coarse-grid lookup and arithmetic. No hostile ever searches the
navigation graph for somewhere to hide.

**Pressure is a budget.** The number of hostiles allowed to advance is capped per difficulty. The
cap is what makes a fight readable: a constant number of things are moving towards you, and the
rest are angles you have to respect.

**Information travels, omniscience does not.** A hostile knows what it saw and what somebody told
it, and being told costs it an extra beat on target. The cascade is bounded by a hop count so one
contact cannot wake the building.

**Nothing may cost per-step work proportional to the world.** Every mechanic here is either
event-driven (impacts, shouts, corpse discovery), throttled to a few hertz, or baked. The
raycast budget is a hard two per hostile per step, enforced by a gate rather than by convention.

## 2. Cover combat

### 2.1 The baked cover map — `src/ai/cover.js`

A cover point is a nav node just outside a shot-blocking obstacle, near enough to one **end** of
that obstacle's face that the occupant can lean past the edge to shoot. That definition is what
makes a single point usable in both directions: standing on the point, the obstacle is between the
occupant and the far side; stepped out along `lateral` by `peek`, the firing line is clear.

The bake is driven by the collider list, not by nav nodes. For each obstacle that stops bullets,
blocks movement, is static, and stands at least 1.0 m tall, its four faces are walked one nav cell
(0.5 m) at a time along a line 0.55 m out from the face, and each step is looked up in the nav grid
by cell. That is 473 obstacles instead of 21 985 nodes.

Each point stores where to stand, the bearing into the obstacle, the bearing along the face towards
the near end, how far to lean, an 8-sector bitmask of the bearings it is protected from, and whether
the obstacle hides a standing man (≥1.7 m) or only a crouching one.

| Bake statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Obstacles considered | 473 |
| Cover points | 1974 (1625 full height, 349 low) |
| Distribution | 1163 ground floor, 811 upper floor |
| Runtime lookup cells (3 m coarse grid) | 236 |
| Cover bake time | 10.3 ms |
| Nav bake time it rides on | 90 ms (160 ms on a loaded box) |

Ten milliseconds on a bake that already costs 90–160 ms depending on how busy the machine is, once
per mission load. The point counts above are identical across runs — the bake is deterministic and
reads only static geometry; only the timings move. Runtime queries
(`near()`) touch at most a 7×7 block of coarse cells at the 8.5 m radius combat uses, and allocate
nothing beyond a reusable result array.

### 2.2 Fighting from it

`_maintainCover()` runs behind a 1.5–2.4 s cooldown, scores the candidates within 8.5 m, and takes
the best. Scoring balances five things: distance from the target against the hostile's *ideal*
engagement range, distance from where it currently stands, a bonus for full height, the assigned
flank bearing when the hostile is holding rather than pushing, and a heavy penalty (−7) when the
firing line from the lean-out position runs through smoke. Suppression shifts the preference
towards full-height cover and away from low.

The chosen point is verified with exactly **one** ray from the lean-out eye position to the target
— and only if perception left a ray in the budget. A point whose lean-out cannot see the target is
rejected and retried 0.4 s later rather than committed to.

Micro-moves inside cover (tuck ↔ lean) bypass A\* entirely: `wantDirect` steers straight when the
destination is within 2.8 m and on the same level. That is what keeps the re-path rate flat during
a firefight (§10.2).

### 2.3 Peek rhythm and suppression

While tucked on the point the weapon is down and no shot is taken; stepped out, the hostile aims
and fires. The rhythm alternates on `peekT` with windows drawn from difficulty. Suppression —
accumulated from bullet impacts within 2.6 m and from damage taken, decaying at 0.42/s — shortens
the look and lengthens the tuck, but **never stops the rhythm**. A hostile that stays down for as
long as the trigger is held reads as broken rather than as cautious, so even a fully pinned one
snaps out to trade a burst. That is the whole purpose of the `pinnedPeek` / `pinnedTuck` pair in
§9.1: `pinnedPeek` is the floor on the look, not a suppression of it.

Measured on Operator against a player firing 200 ms bursts every 600 ms from 8.5 m
(`cover-fight--trace.json`, 106 samples at 10 Hz):

```
posture  TTTTTPPPPPPTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTPPPPPPTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTPPPPPPTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTPPPPPPTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTTT
         T = tucked behind the obstacle, P = leaning out
```

In cover for 106 of 106 samples, 82 of them tucked, suppression peaked at 0.97 (saturated — the
incoming fire never let up), 10 rounds returned during the four lean-outs. The cover point it chose
(index 972) is full height; it tucks to `[13.25, 14.75]` and leans to `[13.25, 14.15]`, a 0.6 m

## 3. Coordinated pressure — `src/ai/squad.js`

At most `ai.maxPushers` hostiles may advance on the player at once. `coordinate(mission)` is called
from every hostile's update but throttled through a `WeakMap` keyed on the mission to run at 3.3 Hz,
so the work is one sort of the *engaged subset* a few times a second — never a search, and nothing
written onto the mission object.

Only hostiles actually in combat state hold a token, which is what enforces "no global omniscience":
a hostile that has neither seen the player nor been told is not in the pool at all. Anyone inside
4 m fights regardless of the budget — backing a hostile off in the open next to the player looks
broken, not disciplined. Losing contact drops both the role and the assigned angle, so neither
follows a hostile into its next engagement.

Holders take distinct bearings around the target — alternating sides, widening by 0.5 rad per pair
— and rotate through them on `holdRepositionSec`, invalidating their cover choice so the
reposition is a real move rather than a change of facing.

Five hostiles ringed around one target on Operator (`pressure-cap--tokens.json`, 10 samples over
8 s):

| | value |
|---|---|
| Engaged | 5 |
| Cap (`maxPushers`) | 2 |
| Pushers / holders | 2 / 3 |
| Samples over the cap | **0** |
| Holder bearings over time | `-49°,195°,-103°` → `195°,-103°,-93°` → `-129°,-103°,-93°` → `-129°,-217°,-93°` |

The bearing column is the reposition working: holders swap angles without ever becoming pushers.
`pressure-cap--pressure.png` frames the two hostiles holding push tokens advancing side by side.
The ring the scenario spawns is wide enough that no single frame holds all five, so the JSON is
where the whole group lives.

## 4. Information propagation

### 4.1 Shouts

A hostile entering combat calls the contact out. The message rides the mission's existing `noise`
dispatch — `bus.emit('noise', { type: 'shout', … })` — so no wiring was added to `mission.js`, and
`hearNoise` forks to `_hearShout` on the type. Hostages ignore it (they only react to gunshots,
glass and flashes), which is correct.

A shout reaches an ally within `shoutRadius` if the ally can see the caller **or** shares its
`homeRoom`. Recipients that get a position enter combat with `told = true` and pay
`+0.35 × enemyReaction` extra aim delay — being told is not the same as seeing. Recipients that get
no position (Recruit, which withholds it) go and look where the shout came from instead.

Three bounds keep the cascade from waking the building: a 2.5 s per-hostile shout cooldown, a hop
count (`shoutHops`) carried on the event and incremented per relay, and the fact that a hostile
already in combat updates its last-known and does **not** re-shout. The LOS check is ray-gated; if
the step's rays are spent it falls back to 60 % of the shout radius, so information still travels
but only to the genuinely close.

### 4.2 Corpse discovery

`_checkBodies()` runs at 2 Hz. A dead ally within `corpseRadius`, roughly in front (or within
2.5 m in any direction), confirmed by one ray, escalates: suspicion to 1, investigate the body, then
search around it, and a `kind: 'body'` shout so neighbours come and look too. The body id goes into
`knownBodies` — and into the `knownBodies` of everyone who hears the shout — so the chain fires once
per body per hostile and cannot loop.

This check runs **before** perception in the update, which is not cosmetic: perception spends both
rays whenever the player is out of sight (eye ray, then the chest fallback), so in the other order a
hostile walks past its dead colleagues forever. Corpse discovery needs 1 ray at 2 Hz; perception
needs 120 Hz, so it barely notices.

The chain, Operator (`corpse-chain--trace.json`): a lobby patrol is killed quietly, and a neighbour
7 m away with a clear view of it is put back on patrol with no memory of the kill.

```
1.8 s  investigate  (suspicion 1, heading for the body)
3.8 s  search       (arrived — closest approach 0.80 m — now checking around it)
17.8 s guard        (search over, wary refreshed to 60 s, body remembered)
45.8 s guard        (trace ends with 31 s of wary still on the clock)
```

`corpse-chain--found-body.png` shows the dead trooper on the lobby floor with the witness walking in.

## 5. Search behaviour

Losing the player queues the last-known position followed by 2–4 plausible places to look, drawn
first from the cover map around that position (the places a person would actually hide) and then
from the navigation graph. Each spot gets a scanning dwell from `searchDwell`; spots that came from
the cover map always get a crouch check, open-floor spots get one on `crouchCheckChance` — checking
behind a crate means going down to look behind it. When the queue empties, the hostile returns to
patrol or guard with `wary` set: faster scan sweep, a wider vision cone (66° instead of 60°),
suspicion gain multiplied by `waryAlertness`, and patrol speed up from 0.42× to 0.55×.

Three robustness fixes came out of watching this run, all of them the same underlying problem:

**The last-known position is where the *player* stood.** A 0.37 m hostile capsule frequently cannot
get there. The head of the queue is now snapped onto the navmesh, so the hostile checks the nearest
place it can stand.

**The nav bake calls furniture tops walkable.** It marks any surface it can stand a 0.24 m probe on,
which includes desk and cabinet tops, and the character mover only steps 0.4 m. Both level filters
that hand out destinations are now one step wide instead of 1.5–1.6 m: `CoverMap.near()` and
`NavGrid.randomNodeNear()`. Before this, a hostile on the lobby floor would happily select a cover
point on top of the reception desk and grind against the desk return until a watchdog fired.

**A spot it stalled next to still counts as checked.** The progress watchdog drops a spot after
2.2 s of no ground covered; if the hostile is within 2.6 m when that happens it now gets the full
dwell and the crouch check, because it *has* effectively looked the thing over. Previously it got a
0.5–0.9 s glance and hurried off, which is exactly the robotic beat the deliverable is about.

I also gave `investigate` the give-up it was missing. Several stimuli hand over a position no capsule
can reach — a shout from the floor above, a body wedged behind a desk — and without a budget the
hostile grinds against the geometry indefinitely. It now carries a distance-scaled budget
(`min(30, 7 + distance × 1.4)` seconds) plus a 3 s no-progress watchdog, and on expiry searches from
where it stands. That is what makes the state machine guaranteed to terminate in wary patrol.

Operator, isolated (`search-trace--trace.json`, 110 samples over 55 s):

```
 5 s  combat   contact
 9 s  search   3 spots queued, first is the snapped last-known
       ... dwell, scan, crouch-check at a cover-derived spot ...
26 s  guard    wary 60 s, 24.7 m walked
```

`search-trace--searching.png` and `search-trace--crouch-check.png` (kneeling beside the lobby
entrance, weapon up).

## 7. Door tactics

A closed door across a hostile's path is scanned for at 5.5 Hz. In combat, investigate or search the
hostile stops for 0.3–0.5 s, opens, then enters with a 22 % speed boost for 0.85 s and the weapon
up. Patrolling and guarding hostiles open without the beat — there is nothing on the other side to
be careful about yet.

Doors a hostile opened are remembered (up to four). While patrolling or guarding, the oldest one is
considered at 0.83 Hz: between 2.4 m and 8 m away there is a 30 % chance per check of shutting it
again — never in combat, never a door it did not open itself, and never onto somebody standing in
the doorway (the player or another hostile within 1.8 m vetoes it). Beyond 11 m it is forgotten
instead. `door.close()` emits no noise and the nav graph excludes doors, so nothing downstream
notices.

`doors--trace.json`, one hostile driven at `door-courtyard` from 2.7 m:

```
0.6 s  closed   pause 0.29 s at 1.57 m out
0.9 s  opening  entry boost 0.84 s, 1 door remembered
1.6 s  open     walks through
6.5 s           second door: pause 0.38 s, then boost 0.84 s from 6.9 s, 2 remembered
13.8 s closing  the patrol shut the first one behind itself (2 remembered -> 1)
14.5 s closed
```

## 8. Hostage escort under fire

Three rules hold at all times while following, and they are stated at the top of `hostage.js`
because they are the contract:

**The follow never stops for longer than a flinch.** A round landing within 1.7 m buys a
0.35–0.8 s freeze with a 1.8 s cooldown. A sustained volley cannot pin a hostage in a doorway.

**Danger lowers the profile, not the commitment.** Gunfire, glass or a flash within 10 m, or any
hostile in combat within 10 m (scanned at 2.5 Hz, never per step), puts the hostage into a
crouch-walk: kneel pose, 75 % speed. Taking a hit forces 4 s of it.

**Distance raises the catch-up speed.** 2.4 m/s inside 6 m, 3.4 above 6, 4.0 above 10, 4.6 above 16.
A player who sprints ahead is followed, not lost.

Stuck recovery was tightened to two stages: 0.6 s of no movement triggers a 0.5 s sidestep
perpendicular to the current heading (alternating sides), and only 1.5 s triggers a fresh route.
Waiting hostages cower while `dangerT` is up and stand when it runs out.

Both hostages walked from the lobby fight to the extraction garage
(`escort-underfire--trace.json`):

| sc-mid | 5 | following | yes | 3.7 m |
| sc-west | 4 | following | yes | 5.0 m |

Crouch-follow observed throughout, flinches observed, and the gap never exceeded 5 m across a

## 10. Performance discipline

### 10.1 Raycast budget — the hard one

`RAY_BUDGET` is 2 per hostile per simulation step, enforced by `_rayGate()`. The counter rolls over
on `mission.timer` rather than at the top of `update()`, and that detail is the whole fix: noise and
shout dispatch reach a hostile from *another* hostile's update, so a counter reset in `update` let
those rays escape the budget entirely. It did — the worst case measured 3 before the gate moved.

Every optional ray asks the gate first and does something cheaper when the answer is no: the cover
verification ray is skipped (the point is committed unverified and the peek/tuck feedback loop
corrects it), the corpse LOS check waits for its next 2 Hz tick, the shout LOS check falls back to a
distance test, and the footstep material ray reuses the last sample.

Measured across a building-wide fight, 14 live hostiles, 480 steps (`ray-budget--perf.json`):

| | value |
|---|---|
| Rays per hostile per step | **0.825** |
| Worst single step, any hostile | **2** |
| Hostiles over budget | **none** |
| A\* requests per hostile per second | 0.75 |
| Sim cost per step | 1.22 ms |

### 10.2 Re-path rate (NS-7) and the A\* budget (NS-8)

Both intact. `mission.findPath`'s per-step budget of 3 is untouched, and the backoff still gates
every request. Two things in my code protect it:

* `pathGapT` is a hard floor under the re-path interval, independent of `repathT`. Anything that
  legitimately zeroes `repathT` (a fresh alert, arriving at a waypoint, stuck recovery) still cannot
  produce requests faster than ~2.9/s.
* The clamp-teleport safety net takes `repathT = Math.max(repathT, 0.2)` rather than assigning 0.2,
  so the longer backoff always wins.

Combat micro-moves (tuck ↔ lean, strafe) steer directly instead of pathing, which is why the
measured rate *fell* relative to baseline: 0.75 requests per hostile per second during a
building-wide fight, against a test limit of 4 for the worst individual and 2 for the mean.
`PW-25 hostiles chasing across floors respect their re-path backoff` — green.

### 10.3 Frame budget

`PW-25 the stepped simulation stays inside its frame budget on quality=low`, three runs:

```
PW-25 sim step: min 0.431 ms, median 0.808 ms, max 1.162 ms, 14 live hostiles   (only test running)
PW-25 sim step: min 0.527 ms, median 1.116 ms, max 1.868 ms, 14 live hostiles   (full suite, 1 worker)
PW-25 sim step: min 0.489 ms, median 1.021 ms, max 2.321 ms, 14 live hostiles   (full suite, 2 workers)
```

That is 10–13 % of the 8.33 ms available at 120 Hz.

**A measurement caveat for the lead.** One earlier full-suite run on the default two workers
reported a median of 2.474 ms against an assertion of `< 3`, which looks alarming and is not: it is
the second Playwright worker competing for the same cores. The final verification run, same two
workers, came back at 1.021 ms, so that number was contention rather than a trend — and the spread
between the two solo runs above is other agents' test runs on the same box. I instrumented the running build to
split the step and the breakdown is healthy — 16 character rigs 0.43 ms, all 14 hostile AI updates
0.71 ms (0.05 ms each), world queries 0.19 ms across 26 queries per step at 7.2 µs each, raycasts
0.025 ms across 3.9 per step at 6.5 µs each. If the assertion ever trips in CI, the first thing to
check is worker contention, not the AI. It may be worth pinning that one test to `--workers=1`.

Everything O(hostiles × navNodes) is baked or event-driven: cover extraction runs once at bake from
the collider list, the impact listener is one module-level subscription that walks only live
hostiles, squad coordination is throttled to 3.3 Hz and sorts only the engaged subset, corpse checks
run at 2 Hz, danger scans at 2.5 Hz, door scans at 5.5 Hz.

Two notes on the harness. It stubs `@vite/client` exactly as `tests/helpers/game.js` does — a hot
update to a module without an accept handler reloads the page and destroys the execution context
mid-scenario. And rendering is on demand: under SwiftShader a frame drawn as the only work in an
event-loop task costs seconds, so `advanceTime` never renders and `shot()` draws one real frame
immediately before the screenshot.

Posture shots are framed from a navmesh vantage point (`spot()`/`vantage()`/`look()`), not from the
player's eye. A hostile 8 m down a corridor is a few dozen pixels tall in a 1920×1080 first-person
frame, and these scenarios are entirely about what its body is doing. The vantage finder wants a
standable node on the same level whose line to the subject is clear both visually *and* physically,
and it took two goes to get there. A fixed-radius orbit camera ends up outside the building or
inside a ceiling slab, so the first version of the pressure-cap shot was a photograph of the
skyline; moving to navmesh nodes fixed that, but "clear line of sight" alone then chose a spot out
in the snow looking in through the curtain wall, because glass stops movement and not sight. Adding
the physical-clearance test put the camera back in the room with its subject, and it falls back to
the sight-only test rather than failing a scenario over a camera.

## 13. For the lead

**No new audio bark kinds needed.** Everything uses the existing `hostileAlert`, `hostileCombat`,
`hostageFear`, `hostageRelief` and `audio.mech('magin', …)`. If there is appetite later, the two
places where a distinct bark would add most are the corpse-discovery shout (currently
`hostileAlert`) and a "pinned/suppressed" grunt, which would make §2.3's rhythm audible instead of
only visible.

**Two map observations, both outside my files, neither blocking:**

1. **Several named checkpoints are not standable.** `lobby` puts the player on top of the reception
   desk (feet at y = 1.16); `cubes` puts the capsule inside a structural column and physics
   depenetrates it onto the roof at y = 6.3. This is a QA-tooling problem rather than a gameplay one
   — the player never teleports in a real session — but it silently invalidates any scenario that
   measures distances, and it cost me most of a debugging pass. `tools/capture-ai.js` works around it
   with a `standAt()` helper that teleports, then walks nearby nav nodes until the capsule settles on
   the right floor. Worth fixing at the source, in `CHECKPOINTS`.
2. **The nav bake marks furniture tops walkable.** It accepts any surface that clears a 0.24 m probe,
   so desk and cabinet tops become nav nodes (`21 985` nodes include a full grid on top of the lobby
   reception desk at y = 1.16), while the character mover only steps 0.4 m. I contained this inside my
   own domain by narrowing every level filter that hands out a destination to one step, but the graph
   itself still contains islands nothing can reach. If `_clearance()` also required the node to be
   reachable from a neighbour within `MAX_STEP`, those islands would disappear and the graph would
   shrink. That is a `navgrid.js` change I own, but it affects path costs everywhere, so I would
   rather it land in its own work package with the perf suite re-run than ride along on this one.
