## 1. What the combat model does now

The through-line of this work package is that every source of inaccuracy or view movement should be
something the player can learn and then beat. Randomness that the player cannot predict was removed
from the shooting path entirely; what is left is deterministic and repeatable, and each weapon has a
shape you can memorise.

Three systems carry that:

**Recoil is a pattern, not a shake.** Each weapon has an authored `pattern` in `defs.js` — a list of
`[pitchMul, yawMul]` steps walked one entry per shot, with the last entry repeating for the rest of
the magazine. Each kick is split into two parts. The *punch* (`kickPitch`/`kickYaw`) is the snappy
component that settles quickly on its own; the *climb* (`climbPitch`/`climbYaw`) accumulates and is
held while the trigger is down, only recovering after `recoil.hold` seconds. The camera and the
viewmodel read the sum through the existing `recoilPitch`/`recoilYaw`/`rollKick` fields. Both parts
decay exponentially towards zero, which is what guarantees recovery approaches the pre-fire aim
without ever crossing it — verified below. Pattern position rewinds to step 0 after
`recoil.resetMs` of trigger idle, so a burst-discipline player starts every burst from the same
known first kick.

**Spread rewards standing still.** `spreadDeg(moveSpeed, crouched)` keeps its signature. The
first shot from a settled ADS stance is effectively a laser (0.05°–0.3° depending on the weapon),
because bloom is applied *after* the shot resolves — see the ordering note in §7. Bloom accumulates
per shot to a per-weapon cap, holds briefly, then decays. Movement is scaled by a 1.4-exponent curve
so a slow creep costs almost nothing and a run costs a lot; being airborne is punished hard and
aiming barely helps.

**ADS is a per-weapon commitment.** `adsMs` drives both the visual blend and the accuracy
interpolation, so a slow weapon is genuinely slower to bring to bear rather than just slower to look
at. Sights drop 1.35× faster than they come up. `adsMoveMul` slows the player while aiming, blended
over the same curve, and is exposed to the player controller through `arsenal.aimMoveMul`. The LR-8
adds a deterministic Lissajous breath sway on `arsenal.scopeSwayX/Y`, steadied by holding Shift
against a depleting budget.

### Sign convention worth knowing

Recoil patterns are authored in **screen space**: a positive `yawMul` kicks to the player's *right*.
The camera's yaw *decreases* when you turn right, so `_kick()` negates the yaw step on the way into
the offset. This is the one place the two conventions meet, and it is commented in
`src/weapons/weapon.js`. The screenshots in §3 confirm the authored direction and the on-screen
direction now agree.

### Player armour

Armour is region-aware. `ARMOR_BYPASS` is the fraction of a hit that goes around the plate
entirely: `{ head: 0.6, torso: 0.26, legs: 0.8 }`. The plate eats `ARMOR_SOAK` (0.8) of what is
left, at a cost of `ARMOR_WEAR` (1.15) plate points per point absorbed. A helmet is deliberately not
modelled, so a head shot always gets most of the way through.

### Sound profiles

Signatures were pushed apart along frequency and duration rather than volume: the P9 is a high thin
crack (4300 Hz, 32 ms tail), the K5 is the shortest and quietest per shot because it fires the most
(2500 Hz at high Q, 200 ms tail), the HC-4 is a mid boom with body, the S-12 is the lowest body
(132 → 40 Hz) with a 950 ms bloom, and the LR-8 has the sharpest crack over a 1.4 s tail.

There is no limiter on the master bus, so the profile gains have to fit under unity themselves. The
worst case is the fully-coherent sum of body + crack + tail across an entire sustained string,
accounting for each component's own attack and decay:

| Weapon | cycle | coherent peak @ max volume | at default volume (0.8 × 0.8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| P9 | 182 ms | 0.814 | 0.521 |
| K5 | 75 ms | 0.657 | 0.421 |
| HC-4 | 94 ms | 0.926 | 0.593 |
| S-12 | 1442 ms | 0.945 | 0.605 |
| LR-8 | 1333 ms | 0.963 | 0.616 |

Everything clears unity even with every volume slider maxed, and the loudness order is the intended
one (LR-8 > S-12 > HC-4 > P9 > K5). This bound is pessimistic — the body is an oscillator while the
crack and tail are noise, so they do not actually sum in phase. The HC-4 and LR-8 profiles were
trimmed slightly (HC-4 body 0.50 → 0.48, crack 0.36 → 0.35; LR-8 body 0.56 → 0.53, crack
0.44 → 0.42, tail 0.34 → 0.33) to buy that headroom.

### Movement

| Constant | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| `ACCEL_GROUND` | 26 | takes a beat to get going — tactical weight |
| `DECEL_GROUND` | 34 | braking is sharper than launching, so peeking out and stopping is precise |
| `ACCEL_AIR` | 5.5 | micro air-control only |
| `AIR_STEER_LIMIT` | 0.42 | a jump may redirect at most 42 % of walk speed |
| `JUMP_VELOCITY` | 4.4 | — |
| `LAND_LOCK` | 0.24 s | jump is refused this long after touchdown: no bunny-hop chaining |
| `LAND_STEADY` | 0.20 s × (0.5 + fall factor) | post-land slowdown to 0.72× so hops cannot be chained into speed |
| walk (Shift) | 0.50× speed | against a `noise` radius that scales with the fall/step |
| crouch | 0.42× speed | eye 1.60 → 1.12 m |

Landing dip scales with impact speed (`0.03 + k² × 0.2` for `k` over a 3.2 m/s threshold), so a
kerb barely registers and a storey buckles the knees. Footsteps are now driven off `bobPhase`
rather than a separate distance accumulator: `|sin(bobPhase)|` bottoms out every π, and a step fires
on each valley crossing, which means the footstep you hear is the footstep you see.

## 3. Recoil pattern traces

`tools/capture-combat.js recoil-trace` fires from a fixed pose at a flat drywall face and marks
every impact with a quad coloured by shot order — **cyan is the first shot, red is the last**. The
markers and the camera framing both scale with the cluster, so a P9's few centimetres and the
HC-4's near-metre climb are both legible.

Finding a good wall turned out to be most of the work. The search now requires one *unbroken*
collider across the whole patch the pattern walks over (otherwise it happily picks a partition with
a glass door in the middle of it), and it aims slightly above the horizon on purpose — furniture and
wall-mounted screens do not block shots, so a cluster at desk height is correct in the data but
hidden behind a prop in the screenshot.

Measured at 6.96 m, ADS, standing still, firing at each weapon's own rate cap:

| Weapon | shots | vertical span | horizontal span | final drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P9 | 8 | 0.055 m | 0.044 m | tight cluster, slight alternating wobble |
| K5 | 16 | 0.449 m | 0.229 m | rises fast, then wanders both ways |
| HC-4 | 13 | 0.865 m | 0.566 m | climbs, then drifts right |
| S-12 | 32 (4 shells) | 0.457 m | 0.402 m | pellet cloud, dense centre |
| LR-8 | 4 | 0.019 m | 0.033 m | single hole |

Screenshots (all read and reviewed):

- `artifacts/shots/recoil-trace--pattern-halcyon-hc4.png` — the clearest read of the whole set: a
  cyan column rising vertically, then bending right into the red end. This is the pattern the brief
  asked for and it is directly legible as a shape to counter.
- `artifacts/shots/recoil-trace--pattern-boreal-k5.png` — a tight cyan rise at the bottom breaking
  into scattered red at the top. The lateral jitter is doing exactly its job: controllable for the
  first few rounds, a spray after that.
- `artifacts/shots/recoil-trace--pattern-karst-p9.png` — one small clump. Eight rounds at max tap
  rate stay inside 5.5 cm at 7 m.
- `artifacts/shots/recoil-trace--pattern-vanta-s12.png` — four shells of buckshot: a dense core with
  a wide fringe, each shell nudged by the previous punch.
- `artifacts/shots/recoil-trace--pattern-meridian-lr8.png` — four rounds in one hole.

**Repeatability:** the same burst fired twice from the same pose across a full mission reset lands
in bit-identical positions (27 vs 27 impacts, deep-equal). The pattern is a skill to learn, not a
dice roll.

The P9 and LR-8 clusters being nearly a single hole is the correct answer rather than a missing
feature: both cycle slowly enough that the climb fully recovers between shots, so their recoil is a
*visual* and *recovery-time* cost, not a dispersion one. Note that an earlier version of this
scenario tapped slower than the weapons allow, which hid even the P9's small climb — the loop now
fires at each weapon's real rate cap.

## 4. Recoil envelope and recovery

`recoil-envelope` samples `recoilPitch`/`recoilYaw`/`rollKick` through a burst and then through the
release, which is the direct test of "recovery pulls the camera back toward, not past, the pre-fire
aim".

| Weapon | peak pitch | peak abs yaw | peak abs roll | settles in | overshoots aim? | pattern steps used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P9 | 1.92° | 0.21° | 0.09° | 200 ms | **no** | 6 |
| K5 | 2.87° | 1.21° | 0.16° | 400 ms | **no** | 16 |
| HC-4 | 7.12° | 3.77° | 0.32° | 560 ms | **no** | 13 |
| S-12 | 3.94° | 0.37° | 0.32° | 40 ms | **no** | 1 |
| LR-8 | 4.95° | 0.18° | 0.14° | 40 ms | **no** | 6 |

No weapon's pitch ever crosses below zero after the trigger is released, so the view always returns
to the original aim from one side. Total view offset is capped at 10.9° (`MAX_RECOIL_PITCH`) and the
worst real case is the HC-4 at 7.12°, so the ceiling is headroom rather than a clamp the player
feels. The S-12 and LR-8 read as "settling in 40 ms" only because their cycle times are longer than
their own recovery — by the time you can fire again, you already have.

## 5. ADS timing and scope sway

`ads-timing` steps 25 ms at a time from the moment the aim button goes down.

| Weapon | `adsMs` | measured settle | hip spread | ADS spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P9 | 140 | 150 ms | 1.50° | 0.16° |
| K5 | 180 | 200 ms | 1.90° | 0.30° |
| HC-4 | 210 | 225 ms | 1.50° | 0.14° |
| S-12 | 200 | 200 ms | 4.20° | 3.00° |
| LR-8 | 380 | 400 ms | 4.00° | 0.05° |

Measured settle tracks `adsMs` to within one sample interval, so the number in `defs.js` is the
number the player experiences. The pistol snaps up in 140 ms and the LR-8 takes 380 ms, as
specified.

**Scope sway (LR-8):** free-breathing amplitude 0.160° × 0.099°, dropping to 0.017° × 0.013° with
Shift held — a 9× steadying, against a 3.2 s budget that refills at 1.1 s per second. The figure is
a deterministic Lissajous (`rateX` 0.9, `rateY` 1.27, offset 1.1 rad) driven off accumulated arsenal
time, so a held breath traces the same path every attempt. `artifacts/shots/feel-frames--lr8-scoped.png`
shows the scoped view is clean and the reticle reads.

## 6. Penetration

Penetration is fully deterministic — the rng jitter that used to be applied to the continuing ray
was removed. Material rules live in one table in `ballistics.js`:

| Material | min penetration | max thickness | damage multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| drywall | 0.25 | 0.25 m | 1.00 |
| wood | 0.45 | 0.20 m | 0.85 |
| tile | 0.55 | 0.16 m | 0.75 |
| snow | 0.20 | 0.90 m | 0.90 |
| glass | always, for a 0.92× toll | — | — |
| concrete / metal / stone | never | — | — |

Damage surviving one wall is `retention(pen) = 0.22 + pen × 0.6`, times the material multiplier.
Thickness is measured *along the ray*, so a partition hit at a glancing angle is genuinely harder to
punch through than one hit square on. Caps are one solid wall plus three glass panes per shot.

Measured against a 110 hp trooper standing 1.6 m behind a 0.16 m drywall partition:

| Weapon | wall impacts | hit through? | damage through | of base | hp left |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P9 | 2 | yes | 12.9 | 46 % | 97.1 |
| K5 | 2 | yes | **8.6** | **43 %** | 101.4 |
| HC-4 | 2 | yes | 21.1 | 64 % | 88.9 |
| S-12 | 8 (all pellets stopped) | no | 0 | — | 110 |
| LR-8 | 2 | yes | **83.0** | **79 %** | 27.1 |

This is the spread the brief asked for: the LR-8 remains a serious threat through an office
partition (one shot takes a trooper to a quarter health), while the K5 poking at a wall achieves
almost nothing. Buckshot does not penetrate at all.

**Exit wounds fire on both faces.** Every penetrating round reports exactly two impacts on the
partition — entry on the near face and an `exit: true` impact on the far face — which is what the
two screenshots document: `penetration--entry-meridian-lr8.png` (corridor side) and
`penetration--exit-meridian-lr8.png` (far side, showing the hole and the hostile that was hit
standing beside it). Down the sights all you can see is wall, so these are framed from off to one
side with the hole marked.

**Controls.** An LR-8 round — the most penetrating in the game — is stopped dead by both metal and
concrete: one impact on the near face, no exit, and the magazine confirms a round was actually
fired. (An earlier version of this control silently fired nothing, because the LR-8's 1333 ms cycle
had not elapsed; the check now asserts the shot happened.)

## 7. Weapon handling

**First-shot accuracy comes from event ordering.** `tryFire()` emits `player-fired` — inside which
the mission resolves the ray — *before* applying bloom and calling `_kick()`. So every round leaves
against the aim as it stood before that shot: the first of a burst is on the crosshair, and each
later one carries exactly the pattern the ones before it laid down.

**+1 chambered round (new).** Weapons marked `chamber` in `defs.js` are closed-bolt. A *tactical*
reload — one with rounds still in the magazine — keeps the chambered round and ends at
`magSize + 1`; the reserve pays for it. Reloading from *empty* does not, because the bolt closed on
an empty chamber. Verified per weapon:

| Weapon | magSize | tactical result | empty result | +1? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HC-4 | 30 | **31** | 30 | yes |
| P9 | 15 | **16** | 15 | yes |
| K5 | 30 | **31** | 30 | yes |
| LR-8 | 10 | **11** | 10 | yes |
| S-12 | 7 | 7 | 7 | no — shell-fed, no bolt to hold a round |

The magazine target is frozen in `reloadCap` when the reload *starts*, so firing during the
animation cannot change what the reload delivers.

**Reload cancel on switch.** Switching weapons mid-reload abandons it and the progress is lost:
nothing enters the magazine and nothing leaves the reserve. Measured across a switch away and back,
magazine and reserve both returned unchanged (10 / 90 → 10 / 90) and a
`weapon-state { state: 'reload-cancelled' }` event fired for the HUD.

**Shotgun interrupt-to-fire — fixed.** The shell-by-shell loop only checked the trigger at the
moment a shell finished seating, so a trigger pull mid-shell was dropped unless the player kept
holding until the shell was in. That reads as unresponsive at exactly the moment responsiveness
matters. A shotgun reload is now abandoned the instant the player asks to shoot, provided there is
something to shoot — the shell being pushed in is dropped rather than seated. Verified: two shells
go in (mag 2 → 3 → 4), the trigger pull leaves the reload immediately and the shot lands (mag 4 → 3).
A `reload-interrupted` event is emitted alongside.

**Dry fire.** Six trigger pulls inside ~0.5 s on an empty magazine produce two clicks, not six: the
0.35 s cooldown throttles the sound, and no reserve is spent (60 → 60, magazine stays 0).

### Bug found and fixed: shotgun pump softlocked the next weapon

While instrumenting the above I hit a reachable, permanent softlock. `needsPump` was not cleared
when a new weapon came into the hands, and the auto-pump branch used `def.pumpMs` unguarded. So:
fire the S-12, immediately switch to the sidearm, and the *pistol* enters a `pump` state with
`stateDur: null`. `stateT >= stateDur` is never true against NaN, so the state machine never
finishes — the weapon can never fire again, for the rest of the mission. Reproduced directly:

```
after firing   {"id":"vanta-s12","state":"idle","needsPump":true,"cd":0.87}
after switch   {"id":"karst-p9","state":"pump","dur":null,"needsPump":true}
3 s later      {"id":"karst-p9","state":"pump","dur":null,"needsPump":true}
pistol mag 15 -> 15 *** BRICKED ***
```

Fixed on both sides: `needsPump` and `fireCooldown` are cleared when a draw completes (and in
`giveWeapon`), and the pump branch requires `def.pumpMs > 0`, clearing the flag instead of building
a NaN duration. After the fix the same sequence leaves the pistol `idle` and firing. Switching away
and back to skip a pump is not an exploit: the S-12's 810 ms swap round trip costs more than its
560 ms pump.

## 8. Damage, armour and the knife

`player.damage(amount, fromPos, region, shooter)` now resolves armour per region. One 40-damage hit
against a fresh 75-point plate:

| Region | bypass | health lost | plate lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| head | 0.60 | **27.2** (68 %) | 14.7 |
| torso | 0.26 | **16.3** (41 %) | 27.2 |
| legs | 0.80 | **33.6** (84 %) | 7.4 |

The torso is where the plate earns its keep and where it wears out fastest; the head is barely
protected. A sustained string of 25-damage torso hits reads:

```
armor/health:  58.0/89.8 -> 41.0/79.6 -> 23.9/69.4 -> 6.9/59.2 -> 0/40.2 -> 0/15.2 -> 0/0
```

While the plate holds, a 25-damage hit costs 10.2 health; the moment it is gone the same hit costs
the full 25. That step change is the feedback moment, and `armor-broken` is emitted **exactly once**
at the transition (verified: 1 event across the whole string) carrying health, hit direction and
region for the HUD. `player-damaged` reports `type: 'bullet'` rather than leaking the region name
into the HUD's damage-type field.

**Knife.** A single strong stab, no light/heavy split, 55 base damage. Measured at close range
against a standing trooper the swing lands in the head region, so 55 × 2.5 = **137.5** — a one-shot
kill from the front on 110 hp. From behind, checked against the target's yaw versus the attack
direction, the ×2 `backstabMul` doubles that to **275**, which stays lethal in one stab against a
170 hp heavy even on a body hit.

## 9. Balance / TTK matrix

Scripted at **12 m**, ADS, standing still, against `freezeAI` targets. Two measurements per pair:
*aimed* re-aims between every round and isolates the damage model; *held* fires from a fixed pose
without re-aiming and therefore shows what the recoil pattern costs.

| Weapon | vs scout (70 hp) | vs trooper (110 hp) | vs heavy (170 hp) | trooper time (held) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P9 (28/hit) | 3 hits | **4 hits** | 7 hits | 0.87 s |
| K5 (20/hit) | 4 hits | **6 hits** | 9 hits | **0.40 s** |
| HC-4 (33/hit) | 3 hits | **4 hits** | 6 hits | **0.30 s** |
| S-12 (buckshot) | 1 shell | **2 shells** | 3 shells | 3.03 s |
| LR-8 (105/hit) | 1 shot | **2 shots** | 2 shots | 2.83 s |

Every target in the brief is met: P9 four body hits, K5 six (but its 800 rpm makes it the fastest
raw TTK at 0.40 s), HC-4 four, LR-8 one body shot taking a trooper to exactly **5 hp** (110 − 105),
and heavies clearly tankier (P9 4 → 7, K5 6 → 9, HC-4 4 → 6).

**Head shots** (×2.5) on a trooper: LR-8 one, P9 two, HC-4 two, K5 three, S-12 two. The LR-8 is a
one-shot head kill and a one-shot-plus-a-poke body kill, which is the intended sniper contract.

**Recoil cost, read off the *held* column.** Short bursts stay on target — the HC-4 killed a trooper
with 3 hits from 3 rounds fired — while longer strings start to wander: against a heavy it needed 4
hits from 5 fired. That is the burst-discipline pressure the pattern is for, and it only bites once
a string runs past about five rounds.

**S-12 range behaviour**, one shell per distance against a trooper:

| Range | pellets on target | damage | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 m | 8 | 128 | **kill** |
| 6 m | 8 | 128 | **kill** |
| 9 m | 8 | 97.1 | 12.9 hp left |
| 12 m | 8 | 75.4 | 34.6 hp left |
| 16 m | 8 | 51.4 | 58.6 hp left |

One shell inside 6 m, two out to about 12 m, and under half damage at 16 m. This sweep isolates
falloff by aiming dead centre; in play the widening cone costs pellets on top of this.

## 12. Notes for the lead

1. **`tests/combat.spec.js:312` needs a one-word rename** (`_clearSightFrom` → `_clearSight`) to
   match Opus 3's enemy refactor, or an alias on their side. Not touched by me — outside my
   ownership, and the failure is unrelated to WP-014.
2. **The shotgun-pump softlock (§7) is worth a regression test** in whichever spec owns weapon
   switching. It is a permanent loss of the ability to shoot from an ordinary two-input sequence, and
   nothing in the current suite would have caught it.
3. **The viewmodel multiplies `recoilPitch` by ~1.8.** With the HC-4 peaking at 7.12° of camera
   pitch, the model swings around 13°, which is visible in `feel-frames--mid-burst.png`. The camera
   figure is inside budget and deliberate; if Fable 4 wants a calmer model mid-burst, that multiplier
   is the knob rather than the recoil numbers.
4. **There is no limiter on the audio master bus.** The profiles now fit under unity on their own
   (§2), but any future weapon added without that check could clip. A `DynamicsCompressorNode` on
   master would make the whole thing robust — that is the lead's file, so flagging rather than
   changing.
