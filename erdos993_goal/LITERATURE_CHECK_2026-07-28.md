# Literature check for Erdős Problem 993

Date checked: 2026-07-28

## Current status

The Alavi--Malde--Schwenk--Erdős conjecture that every tree (and
forest) has a unimodal independence polynomial was still described as
open in the 2026 literature.

The clearest current statement is in Hibi, Kara, and Vien,
*Symmetric and unimodal independence polynomials of trees*,
arXiv:2604.18824.  Its introduction says explicitly that the
conjecture remained open as of April 2026 and cites exhaustive
verification through order 29:

https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18824

Grace M. X. Li's March 2026 preprint proves unimodality for the two
infinite non-log-concave families introduced after the order-26
log-concavity counterexamples, but continues to state the general
tree/forest problem as a conjecture:

https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03025

Brett Reynolds' March 2026 preprint reports exhaustive verification
of all 8,691,747,673 trees through order 29 and gives structural
reductions.  Its dependency table marks both proposed global closure
routes as conditional, and its abstract and discussion state that the
full conjecture is not closed:

https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19100781

The earlier computational paper of Yosef, Mizrachi, and Kadrawi
verified trees through order 20 and likewise stated the problem as
open:

https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06744

## Novelty check for the present fixed-rank theorem

Searches were made for the exact rank-6 expression

\[
12i_6^2-i_5i_6-14i_5i_7
\]

and its component term \(14i_5i_7\), as well as for tree-independence
coefficient inequalities at rank six.  No matching theorem was found
in the sources above or in the broader paper search.

The full Reynolds preprint was inspected, not only its abstract.  Its
proved results concern a mean independent-set-size bound, structural
reductions, subdivision/contraction identities, finite enumeration,
and asymptotics for selected families.  It does not contain the
rank-6 inequality or the terminal-broom Newton certificate developed
here.

This is evidence of novelty, not a substitute for expert review or a
formal MathSciNet/zbMATH search.  Any public claim should therefore
say that the result *appears new* and is awaiting independent
mathematical verification.
