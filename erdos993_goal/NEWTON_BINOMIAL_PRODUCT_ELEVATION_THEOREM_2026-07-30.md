# Newton binomial-product elevation theorem

For a polynomial \(f(n)\), write its Newton polynomial at \(n=0\) as
\[
\mathcal N[f](z)=\sum_{k\ge0}\Delta^k f(0)z^k.
\]
Then for all nonnegative integers \(r,s\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N\!\left[
\binom{n+1}{r+1}\binom{n+1}{s+1}
\right](z)
\succeq
(1+z)(1+2z)
\mathcal N\!\left[
\binom nr\binom ns
\right](z).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\succeq\) denotes coefficientwise inequality.

## Proof

The falling-factorial product identity gives
\[
\binom nr\binom ns
=
\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}
\frac{(r+s-j)!}
{j!(r-j)!(s-j)!}
\binom n{r+s-j}.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
M_{r,s,j}=
\frac{(r+s-j)!}
{j!(r-j)!(s-j)!},
\]
with \(M_{r,s,j}=0\) outside \(0\le j\le\min(r,s)\), then
\[
\mathcal N\!\left[\binom nr\binom ns\right]
=\sum_jM_{r,s,j}z^{r+s-j}.
\tag{2}
\]

By Pascal's identity,
\[
\binom{n+1}{r+1}\binom{n+1}{s+1}
=
\binom n{r+1}\binom n{s+1}
+\binom n{r+1}\binom ns
+\binom nr\binom n{s+1}
+\binom nr\binom ns.
\tag{3}
\]
At degree \(r+s+2-k\), subtracting
\((1+3z+2z^2)\mathcal N[\binom nr\binom ns]\)
from the Newton transform of (3) leaves
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{r,s,k}={}&
M_{r+1,s+1,k}
+M_{r+1,s,k-1}
+M_{r,s+1,k-1}\\
&-2M_{r,s,k}-3M_{r,s,k-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

For \(0\le k\le\min(r,s)\), put
\[
a=r-k,\qquad b=s-k.
\]
Direct cancellation in (4) gives
\[
R_{r,s,k}
=M_{r,s,k}
\frac{\Phi(a,b,k)}
{(a+1)(a+2)(b+1)(b+2)},
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi={}&
a^3b+ab^3+k(a^3+b^3)+2(a^3+b^3)\\
&+2abk(a+b)+3ab(a+b)
+2k^2(a^2+b^2)+5k(a^2+b^2)+6(a^2+b^2)\\
&+2abk^2+10abk+4ab\\
&+k^3(a+b)+7k^2(a+b)+10k(a+b)+4(a+b)\\
&+4k^3+4k^2+8k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Every summand in (6) is nonnegative.

It remains only to check the two possible terminal coefficients.
Assume \(r\le s\), and put \(g=s-r\).  At \(k=r+1\), (4) divided
by \(M_{r,s,r}\) is
\[
\frac{
g^2r+2g^2+gr^2+2gr+2g+2r^2+2r
}{(g+1)(r+1)}
\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]
At \(k=r+2\), the residual is zero when \(g=0\), and otherwise is
\[
\frac{(g+r)!}{(g-1)!(r+1)!}\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
There are no further coefficients.  The case \(s\le r\) follows by
symmetry.  Equations (5)--(8) prove (1).

## Positive-linear-combination corollary

Define the elevation operator on the Newton basis by
\[
\mathcal E\binom nr=\binom{n+1}{r+1}.
\]
Because both multiplication and \(\mathcal N\) are bilinear, (1)
implies
\[
\mathcal N[(\mathcal Ef)(\mathcal Eg)]
\succeq
(1+z)(1+2z)\mathcal N[fg]
\tag{9}
\]
whenever \(f\) and \(g\) have nonnegative Newton coefficients.

This applies directly to shifted binomial path-count atoms.  For
\[
p_{A,K}(n)=\binom{n+A}{K},
\]
Vandermonde's identity gives
\[
p_{A,K}(n)=
\sum_r\binom A{K-r}\binom nr,
\tag{10}
\]
which is Newton-positive for \(A,K\ge0\).  Moreover,
\[
\binom{n+A+1}{K+1}
=\mathcal E p_{A,K}(n)+\binom A{K+1}.
\tag{11}
\]
The last term is a nonnegative constant.  Thus simultaneous
one-step elevation of two unsigned path-count atoms obeys the same
baseline multiplier \((1+z)(1+2z)\), with a coefficientwise
nonnegative remainder.

## Significance for stable \(P_4\)

For the stable path parameterization, increasing \(x\) by one
simultaneously elevates the two path-count factors in every raw
product.  The theorem therefore explains the exact recurrence
\[
F_{x+1}\succeq(1+z)(1+2z)F_x
\]
for every unsigned product.  After the known factor
\((1+z)^{2m+x-1}\) is removed, this becomes
\[
P_{x+1}\succeq(1+2z)P_x.
\]
Increasing either \(c\) or \(m\) adds two stable-path vertices and
therefore predicts \((1+2z)^2\), exactly as observed in both the
\(h\ge1\) and bottom-pair quotient packages.

The remaining issue is no longer the unsigned path-count algebra:
it is to show that the signed residual-moment phase combination is
preserved by the positive elevation remainder.
