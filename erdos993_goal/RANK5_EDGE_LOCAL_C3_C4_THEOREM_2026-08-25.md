# Rank-five edge-local theorem for degree surplus three and four

Date: 2026-08-25

Status: **proved all-order theorem**.  This closes endpoint-degree types

```text
c=3: (1,4), (2,3),
c=4: (1,5), (2,4), (3,3).
```

Together with the earlier edge-local theorems, no endpoint type remains in
the rank-five token-sliding route.  The full component-surplus summation is
recorded separately.  This note alone does not prove connected `Q8` or
Erdős Problem 993.

## 1. Generic edge decomposition

Let `uv` be an edge of an `n`-vertex tree `T`.  Put

```text
p=deg(u)-1,             q=deg(v)-1,             c=p+q,
K=T-{u,v},              R=N_K(u) union N_K(v),
H=K-R,                  h=|H|=n-c-2,
I(H;x)=sum a_j x^j.
```

The `c` vertices of `R` root the components of `K`.  After deleting them,
each component of `H` has a distinguished root: a child of one vertex of
`R`.  Partition these distinguished roots into `c` groups, one group for
each vertex of `R`.

For an independent set `S` of `H`, let

```text
z(S)=number of selected distinguished roots,
r_u(S)=number of unhit groups on the u side,
r_v(S)=number of unhit groups on the v side,
r(S)=r_u(S)+r_v(S).
```

Thus `r_u,r_v` count the boundary roots that remain compatible with `S`.
Since

```text
I(T;x)=I(K;x)+xI(K-R_u;x)+xI(K-R_v;x),                 (1)
```

the contribution of an independent `s`-set of `H` to `i5(T)` is

```text
s=5: 1,
s=4: r+2,
s=3: C(r,2)+r,
s=2: C(r,3)+C(r_u,2)+C(r_v,2),
s=1: C(r,4)+C(r_u,3)+C(r_v,3),
s=0: C(r,5)+C(r_u,4)+C(r_v,4).                        (2)
```

Let `E` be the sum of all terms in (2) below rank five after removing the
constant `2a4` from the rank-four line.  Then

```text
i5(T)=a5+2a4+E.                                        (3)
```

## 2. Prescribed-root extension term

Let

```text
Z=sum_(S in I4(H)) z(S).
```

The prescribed-root incidence injection gives

```text
5a5 >=(h-12)a4+2Z.                                    (4)
```

Consequently

```text
5i5(T) >=(h-2)a4+P,             P:=2Z+5E.             (5)
```

We may discard the nonnegative endpoint-split terms in the last three lines
of (2).  The resulting universal weights are

```text
rank 4: r,
rank 3: C(r+1,2),
rank 2: C(r,3),
rank 1: C(r,4),
rank 0: C(r,5).                                        (6)
```

It remains to prove a pointwise lower bound for the payment generated by
(6).

## 3. The `c=3` pointwise payment

Fix an independent `s`-set of the nondistinguished vertices.  Let `x,y,z`
be the numbers of compatible distinguished roots in its three groups.
Sum the rank-four, rank-three, and lower-rank states lying over this fixed
set.  After subtracting `9` for every resulting rank-four state, the
slacks for `s=4,3,2,1,0` are as follows:

```text
Q4 = 6,

Q3 = 3(x+y+z)+30,

Q2 = 5 sum C(x,2)+15(x+y+z)+5,

Q1 = 7 sum C(x,3)
     +2 sum_(i!=j) C(x_i,2)x_j -3xyz
     +15 sum C(x,2)+5 sum_(i<j)x_i x_j,

Q0 = 9 sum C(x,4)
     +4 sum_(i!=j) C(x_i,3)x_j
     +4 sum_(i<j) C(x_i,2)C(x_j,2)
     -sum_i C(x_i,2)x_jx_k
     +15 sum C(x,3)+5 sum_(i!=j)C(x_i,2)x_j.           (7)
```

The first three are visibly nonnegative.  For `Q1`, pair the ordered
quadratic terms and the pair terms:

```text
2C(x,2)y+2C(y,2)x+5xy =xy(x+y+3).
```

If `xyz>0`, division by `xyz` and the three reciprocal-pair inequalities
give

```text
sum_(pairs) xy(x+y+3) >=6xyz >=3xyz.
```

If one variable is zero, the negative term vanishes.  Hence `Q1>=0`.

For `Q0`, allocate the `4C(x_i,2)C(x_j,2)` terms symmetrically.  For fixed
`i` and the other two indices `j,k`, the part paying the sole negative term
is

```text
C(x_i,2){2[C(x_j,2)+C(x_k,2)]+5(x_j+x_k)-x_jx_k}

=C(x_i,2)(x_j^2+x_k^2-x_jx_k+4x_j+4x_k)>=0.           (8)
```

All unused terms in `Q0` are nonnegative.  Thus every `Q_s` is
nonnegative, and summing over the fixed nondistinguished sets proves

```text
P>=9a4                                                    (c=3). (9)
```

## 4. The `c=4` pointwise payment

Now use four compatible group sizes `x_1,...,x_4`.  After subtracting
`12` per rank-four state, the only negative pattern in the `s=1` row is
one root from each of three groups, with total `-e_3`.  The terms

```text
4 sum_(i!=j) C(x_i,2)x_j+15e_2                         (10)
```

pay it.  Pairing the ordered terms turns (10) into

```text
sum_(i<j)x_ix_j(2x_i+2x_j+11).
```

Allocate half of each pair term to each of the two triples containing that
pair.  Within a positive triple `x,y,z`, even after dropping `11`, the
allocated amount divided by `xyz` is

```text
(x+y)/z+(x+z)/y+(y+z)/x >=6.
```

So the negative `e_3` is paid with room to spare.

In the `s=0` row the only negative pattern is one root from every group,
with total `-4e_4`.  Let

```text
G=sum_i C(x_i,2)
       sum_(j<k; j,k!=i) x_jx_k.
```

The available payment includes `G+5e_3`.  If every `x_i>0`, put
`y_i=1/x_i`.  Exact division by `e_4` gives

```text
(G+5e_3)/e_4
 =1/2 sum_(i!=j) y_j/y_i +7/2 sum_i y_i
 >=6+7/2 sum_i y_i
 >=6.                                                     (11)
```

Here the first inequality pairs reciprocal ratios.  If some `x_i=0`, then
`e_4=0`.  Thus (11) pays `4e_4`.  Every other pattern in the `s=0` row is
nonnegative.  The `s=2,3,4` rows are termwise positive.  Therefore

```text
P>=12a4                                                   (c=4). (12)
```

## 5. Completion of every `c=3,4` edge

If `a4>0`, then `h>=4`.  For `c=3`, equations (5) and (9) give

```text
5i5(T)>=(h+7)a4.
```

The required factor is

```text
(n-2)(n-3)/h=(h+3)(h+2)/h=h+5+6/h <=h+7.
```

For `c=4`, equations (5) and (12) give

```text
5i5(T)>=(h+10)a4,
```

while

```text
(n-2)(n-3)/h=(h+4)(h+3)/h=h+7+12/h <=h+10.
```

If `a4=0`, the edge-local target is immediate.  This proves all five
endpoint types listed at the start.
